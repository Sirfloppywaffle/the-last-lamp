## Unreleased
- **Saltmouth: lift, move, store, salvage, undo, Grid or Free** (Expansion 4, step 1; `expansion/EXPANSION4_RUNBOOK.md` §5; built by Claude, Opus 5.5). In Build, in the square and in the Lodge, a tap on a piece you built now **lifts** it instead of taking it up: it leaves the scene and follows the thumb as a ghost, green where it can stand and red where it can't. Drag anywhere to slide it (it moves from where it is, so the thumb never hides it, and the Keeper stays put), or tap where it goes, and the camera follows the piece so a short screen never hides it under the tray; then **✓ Place**, **✕ Cancel** (exactly back where it stood) or **Store**. Closing Build or the town with a piece lifted puts it back. **Storage** is a tab of the tray: a stored piece keeps what it cost and is set down again for nothing; **Salvage** there gives back all it cost (the old take-up), and a stored headstone keeps its name until salvaged. **Undo** takes back the last 10 deeds of this build session (a piece placed, moved, stored or salvaged, a Lodge floor or wall laid); it won't set a piece back on you, and forgets everything when Build closes. **Grid / Free**: Grid snaps to the tiles as before; Free sets a piece to the art pixel wherever its footprint fits over open ground and clear of other pieces, saving `x, y` beside `c, r` (the tiles it covers most of, which are the tiles it blocks, so nothing that keeps the Head-frame, a Keeper's place or the Lodge's door and shelf clear changes). Paths and fences keep to the tiles. A free piece that would wall anything off is refused like any other. Saved in `ll-town.store` and `ll-town.free`, both optional: an old town loads with nothing stored, on the grid. Developer mode does all of it in its own copy. Two town tips (lifting and storing; Grid or Free), one each time Build opens, and the Saltmouth rules line grows. `buildtools.test.js`; the 14 screens check the tool bar, the lifted bar and Storage. On purpose: `townbuild.test.js` now expects a tap on a piece to lift it (and stores and salvages it for the refund). Balance: none (Salvage refunds in full, as taking up did).

## v15.4 — The Knucklebones
The gambling idea from the expansion brainstorm, built as a room behind the stair doors. Designed with Gemini, then reshaped over three playtests by Criss: first push-your-luck, then a table "visually playing the game", then Cult of the Lamb's rules on a table repainted to match the rest of the game ("I tested it out and I enjoyed it").
- **The Knucklebones**, a new Gamble room. The hooded thing plays Cult of the Lamb's knucklebones for essence the stair would burn. Each side has three columns of three. Roll one bone and set it in a column. Matching bones multiply (two 4s score 16, three score 36). A bone knocks every bone of that number out of the facing column. When either side fills, the higher total wins. The stake is 40 + 20 per floor. A win pays double, safe from the toll, and a win by 20 or more adds a relic. A draw hands the stake back. ## How the collaboration has worked
1. **Relay era (v6–v15):** Criss carried files and FIND/REPLACE patches between Claude and Gemini. Gemini proposed, Claude reviewed and tested on Fold-sized screens, and Criss gave the green light. It worked, but it was slow, every version had to be downloaded, and a stale FIND broke at least once.
2. **GitHub era (now):** one repository, pull requests, critical reviews in both directions, CI tests on 14 screen sizes, and Criss merging as the green light. See `AGENTS.md`.

## Lessons that became rules
They're all in `AGENTS.md` section 6. In short:
- class collisions;
- deferred reads after state is cleared;
- `hash()` arity;
- unit-space scale;
- ghost taps;
- UI covering the board;
- loops that hit newborn creatures;
- FINDs that match the wrong place.
