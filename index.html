A tower defense descent

The Last Lamp

Five floors down. One flame. Everything in the dark wants it out.

How the dark works

  • The lamp burns at the center of every floor. Creatures follow the path toward it, and each one that arrives drains its oil. When the oil runs out, the run is over.
  • You can only build inside light. Candle Wards carry light outward, and the lamp's Wick branch widens its own. The Lamplighter's Signal Candles are the exception: one can stand in the dark within 4 tiles of a lit one.
  • Light has to connect. A candle only shines if it stands in light that leads back to the lamp. Take away a candle in that chain and everything past it is lost. If the light shrinks on its own, anything cut off goes dark and does nothing until the light returns. A Signal Candle's light also leaps to another Signal Candle up to 4 tiles away; a dotted thread shows each leap, and cutting it cuts everything past it.
  • The dark hides them. Out of the light, creatures are only shapes and eyes, and your wards cannot hit them. Shades and Abyssal Stalkers stay hidden even in lamplight. Only candles, bound Watchers, Flare, flare bolts, kindled creatures and a few upgrades unveil them.
  • Upgrades branch. Tap a ward or the lamp to open its three branches. Each can take two of them, and only one can reach its capstone. Everything you build is stripped when the floor ends, and you get half of it back.
  • Targeting. Harpoons, Censers and single-target horrors can aim at the first creature in line, the last, or the strongest.
  • Free undo. Anything you place or upgrade between waves refunds in full if you dismantle it before calling the dark.
  • Call early. Once a wave has finished arriving, you can call the next one while stragglers still walk, for bonus essence.
  • Binding. Some creatures leave a violet sigil when they die. Tap it before it fades and that creature joins your roster. Bound horrors are placed like wards and hit far harder. Slay the Keeper Below and its soul lingers longer than most. Bind it and it fights for you on the last floor.
  • Forks. On some floors the stair splits. They take the darker way, and only light you cast counts, never the lamp's. A gold chevron at the fork shows where they will go. Shades and Stalkers do the opposite and hunt your light. Gnashers and bosses keep the main way. A Weir built on the fork shuts one way; flip it between waves. Watchers fly over it, and a Behemoth breaks it. On these floors you also hold the Will-o’-Wisp: tap a way at a fork and, for eight seconds, everything but a boss follows it there.
  • Heavy creatures. Trench Behemoths cannot be stunned or pushed back, and nothing slows them by more than 15%. Plan to out-damage them.
  • Madness. Every horror you field adds madness. At Whispers the lamp flickers. At Slipping a horror may break loose when a wave begins, and you may start to see eyes in the dark that are not there. When the dark leans in, the lamp shrinks and they move faster.
  • Wards. Seven are yours from the start: Candles, Harpoons, Salt, the Bone Chime, Hook Traps set on the path, the Tithe Bowl that pays for every death near it, and the Hex Effigy that makes everything near it take more damage. The Tar Censer, Galvanic Coil and Whaling Gun can only be won as gifts between floors.
  • Combinations. Lightning does double to anything standing in salt. Once a Chime reaches Deathknell, anything stunned, held or pinned takes 30% more. Traps catch Shades and Stalkers without light. Effigies make every other ward hit harder, and Unmasking shows what the dark hides. The Whaling Gun does double damage to Behemoths, elites and bosses.
  • Kits. Every descent starts with a kit. Each kit builds its own version of some wards and casts its own rites: the Lamplighter's Signal Candles pass light across the dark, its Flare Harpoons light what they strike, and Kindle sets a creature burning and running, lighting the dark around it. You start with the Lamplighter; other kits are earned by deeds down the stair or in the Ember Hall. Wards outside your kit can be found as gifts, in merchants, or off the stair.
  • The stair. After each floor you take a gift, then choose one of three doors: a Merchant, a Shrine with pacts, something unknown, a quiet place to rest, a Reliquary with another gift, the Knucklebones (a game of bones against the hooded thing, for essence the stair would burn), or the Deep Stair straight down to an elite floor that pays a relic.
  • The toll. Your salvage from a floor is safe, but 70% of any other essence burns away when you take the stair. Spend it, or lose it. Bounties also thin out the deeper you go.
  • Omens. Each door shows the omen waiting on the floor behind it: a smaller lamp, a swarm, faster creatures, kills that pay nothing. Choose your risks.
  • Embers. Every descent, won or lost, leaves embers behind. Spend them in the Ember Hall on new kits, relics and stories. Carry the lamp to morning to open the Deeper Dark, where each depth adds a new cruelty.
  • The Grimoire. The book in the HUD holds every creature you have met, every ward and rite, and every relic you have found. The relics you carry sit beside it: tap one to read what it does.
  • Saving. The run saves at every break between waves. Save and quit from the pause menu, then Continue from the title.
  • The board turns. On a wide screen the map lies sideways so it can fill more of it. Rotate your device and it follows.
