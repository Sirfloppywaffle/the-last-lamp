## 2. Rules for every pass

1. **Runbook §2 and §3 hold**: no anti-aliasing, whole-pixel scale, outlines one shade darker than the fill, 16 px a tile, characters 16×24, salt, rot, rust, tallow, eerie and never cute.
2. **The palette is locked.** Every PixelLab call passes the town's colours (`PX` as hex). That is why step 3's art needed no new letters. Only `w`, `x` and `z` are left free, so a new letter needs a reason in the PR. A Keeper's own colours stay on their uppercase letters.
3. **Style references.** Approved pieces are the reference for the next ones:
   - `style_object_id` = the Lodge Armchair for 8-facing objects;
   - `style_images` = the hatch, ladder and window for one-facing pieces.
   Once a pass is approved, its best pieces join the set.
4. **Criss sees it before it goes in.** Post a contact sheet of the candidates, plus the chosen ones at game scale in the device shots. Swapping one is a single-line change, so a bold first pick is fine.
5. **Size: measure, don't cap.** Each pass states its kilobytes in the PR, raw and gzipped. There was a 60 KB cap here; #145 measured what it guarded against and found nothing: A2's +76 KB (+14 KB gzipped, 3% of the download) left load time, the sprite build and the heap unchanged on a folded Fold's viewport (461 against 492 ms to load, 56 against 58 ms for `townSprites()`, 10 MB either way). Trim only when a number moves: load time, the town's frame time in `devices`, or `npm run check`. The one real cost is to whoever edits `index.html`: art rows are long lines, so keep each figure's or piece's rows on its own lines.
6. **Motion has a still.** Anything animated has a still frame for Lite and Reduce Motion (rule 18), and no frame allocates (rule 17).
7. **Keep a log.** Add a row to §6 below for every piece: what it is, its PixelLab object id, the candidate chosen, and any hand fixes.
8. **Budget generations.** Each pass estimates its spend up front and reports it in the PR. Look at `get_balance` before starting.

## 3. Step 4 takes the first part (the art kit)

Step 4 draws about 30 catalogue pieces. Drawing them the new way from the start saves redoing them later. So step 4 also does this:

- **The art kit.** The palette list, the style references (§2.3), and the prompt shape that worked in step 3: "low top-down view, … wet, salt-stained, eerie muted colours", passing the palette.
- **The right tool per piece:**
  - furniture with 4 or 8 facings: `create_8_direction_object`, using the Armchair as its style;
  - wall mounts and small things on surfaces (candles, books, bottles, a skull): `create_1_direction_object`. Size 32 gives 64 candidates and 48 gives 16.
- **Dyes cost no generations.** A colourway is a letter-to-letter swap in code, not new art.
- **Redraw the old four.** The Banker's Desk, Stove, Bookshelf and Ritual Rug get redrawn so they match the Armchair. They are catalogue pieces, so this belongs in step 4.
- **`tools/png2rows.py --sheet out.png`.** The tool writes the contact sheet itself, numbered, at 4×, using only the standard library. That replaces the throwaway script used in step 3. *Built (step 4):* it also gained `--outline X`, which takes an edge pixel of pure dark up to the piece's own darkest letter (§2.1's outline rule).
- *Learned (step 4):* with the Armchair as `style_object_id` the canvas must be at least 34, and the pieces came out 27 to 32 px across, so squeezing them to 16 spoiled them. Over-sized pieces were regenerated at canvas 24 without a style object (brighter, but the right size); wide ones (desk, pew, chart table, chest, coral table) keep 36 and a 2-tile footprint. Wall mounts and small things: 1-direction objects at 16 px (a style image forces its own size, 24); small things are about 10 px, so a candle sits on a desk's slot. Pass a short `item_descriptions` list: a 64-candidate job at size 16 draws your items first and 56 useful extras after.
- **Estimated spend:** 10 to 20 generations a piece, so about 350 to 700 of the month's 2,000.

### Art 1: fire, water, weather (about 150 generations)
- **Flames.** The brazier, street-lamp, sconce, stove grate and banker's lamp get 4 to 8 frame loops from `animate_image`, with a step-sized flicker the lights can follow (`townFlick()`).
- **Water.** The sea along the south shore, the tide-pools and the Cellar's brine get slow loops. The brine's glints stay in code.
- **Rain.** Splash and drip frames, and the Attic window's rain as a loop.
- **Effects.** A puff of dust when a piece is placed or salvaged, from `vfx_workbench` (Full only).
- **Test:** each loop has its still frame, and the frame time in `devices` doesn't rise.
- *Learned (A1):* `animate_image` on the piece itself at 32×32 (1 generation each) gives a usable flicker, but it redraws the body too, so only the flame's zone is kept and the rest is locked to the piece's rows (frame 0 is the input, so the still is the art as it was). Below about 4 px wide a flame comes back as a blob: draw those few frames by hand. On flat textures (sea, brine) it repaints the ground, so keep only its moving highlights, in small specks. Rain, splashes and dust come better from `vfx_workbench` parts (`water_splash`, `dust_puff`), which are free and take the palette as their colour ramp.

### Art 2: the Keepers and the souls (about 400 generations)
- **Pilot first.** Make Ambrose the Lamplighter with `create_character` (4 directions; Criss: not 8) plus `animate_character` (`walking`, `breathing-idle`). Put him side by side with today's rows in the shots. **If he doesn't beat the hand rows at 16×24, stop there and keep the rows.** Faces made of two or three pixels are hard to improve on.
- **If the pilot wins:**
  - every Keeper and the rescued souls get the same treatment, keeping their silhouettes and tools: Jonah's harpoon, the Nameless's hood, Voss's rod;
  - their glowing letters (`em`) stay;
  - `png2rows.py` gains `--kit <name>`, which maps colours to that Keeper's uppercase palette, and `--frames`, which turns one PNG strip into a frame list;
  - the walk goes from 2 poses to 4 to 6 frames.
- **Size:** 12 figures × 4 facings × about 6 frames is the biggest cost. Measure it in the PR, and trim frames before trimming facings.

- *Learned (A2 pilot):* `create_character` at "size 24" draws a figure 27 to 29 px tall (standard mode; size 22 gives about 24 to 26, size 20 about 21); pro mode on the Keeper's own palette (10 generations, 8 directions) comes out 27 tall on a 28 canvas, and both are fitted to 16 by 24 with `png2rows.py --kit <name> --frames N --width 16 --height 24` (a strip loses the same columns and rows in every frame). Template animations (`walking` 6 frames, `breathing-idle` 4, 1 generation a facing) keep the figure but **drop what it holds** in most frames and ignore the palette: the held thing is laid back from the still, frame by frame, and `--kit` puts every colour back on the Keeper's letters. What glows must be marked: gold that is not the lantern (his buckle) goes on a letter of his own that does not glow (`Y`). Pro mode on the palette looks rich at full size but is mottled at 16×24, and its faces read as frightening (Criss, #144): standard mode with big-head proportions reads cleaner. Template animations redraw the head every frame, and at this size a pixel's wobble reads as the face swelling: lock the head to the still in every frame. And: **show Criss the candidates and let him choose before building on one**, then show the pick at game scale.
- *Learned (step 6, the Peddler, #148):* lock the head **after** converting, in letters, not in the PNGs: a strip taller than 24 is narrowed by taking the same rows out of every frame, so a frame that bobs a pixel up has a different row cut from its head, and `figure.test.js` sees two heads. Run the edge rule again after laying the head in. The template walk may move what a figure wears (his pack moved shoulders), not only what it holds: compare it with the still before converting. Spend: 4 candidates, 6 animation generations, 10 for the barrow's batch.
- *Learned (A2, the rest, #145):* 20 candidates (2 to 4 a figure) and two retries Criss asked for "with A (C) as the reference": a v3 turn of the picked front view (1 generation) keeps the figure exactly, fresh standard ones written to match drift; he took one of each. Every template walk and idle dropped or moved what the figure holds, and drew things the still did not have (a face in the Nameless's hood, purple on Agathe's habit, red feet on Voss): so the tool is laid back from the still in fixed rectangles moved with the figure's bob, a dark ring round it so a 1 px shaft keeps its colour through `--kit`'s edge rule; the head is locked to the standing frame; and the palette is built from the standing frames only, so the walk wears the same colours. A held thing the model drew in one view only (Jonah's lamp, Agathe's bell, the Croupier's die, the Toothmonger's pliers) is painted into the others by hand from it. What glows is painted on exact `em` colours, and no colour of the art may sit nearer a glowing letter than one that does not glow (Voss's lenses share his spark's cyan). Each figure gains 12 to 25 letters of its own (`TOWN_KITS[k].pal`, after a comment), since PixelLab's colours are richer than the old hand palettes. Spend: 62 generations. Size: +76 KB raw, +14 KB gzipped, which measured as no change in load, sprite build or memory (§2.5 now measures instead of capping).

### Art 3: scenery (about 700 generations; may split into 3a buildings and 3b ground and props)
- **Buildings.**
  - The Lodge, the Drowned Oar, the five workshops, the houses and the Head-frame come from `create_map_object` or `create_building_kit`.
  - The ruined, restored and lit states come from `create_object_state`, so each workshop's three tiers come from one base.
  - Step 7 then adds 3 palettes each by letter swap, as with the dyes.
- **Ground.** `create_topdown_tileset` at 16 px makes the edges between earth and cobbles, cobbles and deck, and earth and the cliff edge. The 24×24 map and `TOWN_MAP` stay exactly as they are; only the painting changes.
- **Props.** Trees, fences, headstones, braziers and the cemetery railings.
- **Test:** `town.test.js` and `townbuild.test.js` still pass untouched, and every building's door and keep tile is where it was.
- *Learned (A3a, #149):* candidates as 1-direction objects on the town palette (`PX` less `w`, `x`), one `item_descriptions` entry per slot so one job covers several buildings: houses at 80 (`1c9621d2-6f14-432b-9c32-f93ffaea40e3`: Lodge L1–L5, chandler C1–C5, Oar O1–O6), Head-frame and crypt at 80 (`7cddd4cc-74ac-4733-a83d-314dca52c741`: F1–F8, K1–K8), tall workshops at 64 (`6af59318-d147-4d5b-9fbb-b0a3ad5f6816`: belfry B1–B5, menagerie M1–M5, smithy S1–S6), low workshops at 48 (`3badbdaf-3d31-43ee-924c-a659c9a06388`: chandlery E1–E8, Try-Works W1–W8), props at 32 (`d1ea1410-815d-4fbc-a4d9-0f81a4ec0877`: trees T1–T10 are frames 0–7, 30, 31; braziers R1–R5 frames 8–12; railings N1–N5 frames 13–17; headstones G1–G12 frames 18–29; 32 extras P1–P32, frames 32–63, for step 7). Ground: four Wang tilesets (`d3121d50-…`, `29b2f740-…`, `43498f3d-…` standard; `5aee8358-…` pro on the ground's letters); none beat today's hand-coded cobbles at 16 px (standard sets come out purple and flat; the pro set reads as noise), so the ground is A3b. Candidate spend: 75 generations. The sheets are on `claude/shots-custom-scenery`, linked from #149. A neighbouring candidate on the job's sheet bleeds a few pixels into the top of each frame: drop connected blobs under about 60 px before converting. The 80 px houses come out 70 to 76 wide, a good fit for five tiles; the Head-frame and crypt candidates lean three-quarter, which reads fine among the front-on houses. A ruin from `create_object_state` ("keep the same size, shape and layout") keeps the windows where they were, so one set of pane rects serves both; a state can be a few rows taller or shorter, which standing it on its foot absorbs. Lit windows are a letter swap of the drawn panes rather than a state: a state redraws the whole building, and its glow would not match the dark one. Fill enclosed clear pixels inside a building (a see-through belfry showed the earth behind it), but not in a lattice like the Head-frame. Then 8 states at 10 generations each (the Lodge's window and chimney, its ruin, six more ruins): 155 in all.

- *Learned (step 7, the village):* one 32 px job holds 24 variants of four kinds (six `item_descriptions` a kind) and 40 useful extras, ten generations. Corner Wang tiles on a half-tile grid make a path one tile wide a narrow track with rounded ends (the corner shows at the tile's centre), so a boardwalk laid across cobbles reads as a plank with pointed ends; that is the set's own rim. A laid path is a terrain (`TOWN_LAID`, codes in `TERR_SET`), and a cell with two kinds of path takes the highest, the other counting as earth. A set that comes back with a flat earth tile (a standard tileset with two terrains of very few colours) is mapped onto the earth's own tile, pixel for pixel, instead of ranked by tone. Standard mode took `lower_base_tile_id` from G1's earth for four of five tries; the fifth (transition 0, shells) drew only earth.
- *Learned (step 7, Criss's playtest):* a Wang set's full tile must be judged on its own, laid as a 2 by 2: the tile snapped by tone from a set whose planks are the earth's own darkness (plank A tarred, plank C outlined) comes back as earth with an outline, which reads as an empty frame on a lone tile or pair; and a shell set's full tile came out duller than its rim tiles, so a middle looked like cobbles. Both were fixed in the data (plank A and C are plank B's tiles recoloured, tan seams and lighter boards; the shell sets' full tile is built from their four corner interiors), and `townWangSel()` now gives a cell all of one path that path's own full tile. Look at a set as a lone tile, a pair, an L and a 2 by 2 before it is kept. A 1-direction job told to draw a side view returns the front again (twelve of twelve fence candidates); `create_8_direction_object` with the front picture as `reference_image_base64` does turn it (10 generations an object at 32 px, a 34 px result). A depth-extruded or post-stacked front picture reads as a pole or a slab, not a fence.
